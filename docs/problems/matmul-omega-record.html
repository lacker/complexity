Statement

Let omega be the matrix multiplication exponent: the infimum of c such that two n x n matrices can be multiplied in O(n^c) arithmetic operations. Trivially omega <= 3 and omega >= 2, and the long-running conjecture was omega = 2. The problem: publish any bound omega < 9/4, or settle the claimed matching lower bound omega >= 9/4 (below) by refereeing or refuting it. Historically the closing move was structural: escaping the Ambainis–Filmus–Le Gall barrier for the laser method (omega < 2.3725), which the 2026 polynomial route did.

Current record

The record is now omega <= 9/4 = 2.25, far below the laser-method value 2.371339 (Alman, Duan, Vassilevska Williams, Xu, Xu and Zhou, SODA 2025, arXiv:2404.16349).

  • Source: an OpenAI preprint (2 Oct 2026, not refereed) proved omega <= 9/4 over the complex numbers by a route outside the Coppersmith-Winograd framework.
  • Every field: Amanov, van den Berg and Zuiddam (arXiv:2610.30054) give an exposition and a version over every field. Lü (arXiv:2610.18914) handles a separation step in every characteristic, over algebraic closures.
  • Claimed matching lower bound: a second OpenAI preprint (24 Nov 2026, not refereed) claims omega >= 9/4 over every field, for formal arithmetic circuits with division. If correct, omega = 9/4 exactly and this entry is resolved.
    • Lean: its Comparator challenge states omega = 9/4 for division-free programs; the release also proves, outside the challenge, that division does not change the square exponent.
    • Independent audit: an audit by Carlos Toledo (carlostoledo.co/omega) reports a from-source build, Comparator with and without nanoda, an independent kernel replay and negative controls, with verdict CERTIFIED for the formal statement. It checked commit 92b516ef.
    • Statement audit: a statement audit by the author of this change compares the Lean statements with the paper. It did not build the Lean.
    • Not covered: no human refereeing yet. The paper's explicit per-n bound (at least n^(9/4)/2, rounded up, multiplication or division gates at each n >= 2) is not formalized in the release.

Why it matters

Omega is a load-bearing constant across the entire reduction web: transitive closure, context-free parsing, all-pairs shortest paths subroutines, dense linear algebra, and the polynomial-method algorithms cited by several other problems all inherit their exponents from it. Any improvement propagates automatically through dozens of published reductions. Conversely, a clean new barrier theorem prunes the search space for everyone.

Attack surface

Below 9/4: the polynomial route that reached 9/4 is capped by the barrier in arXiv:2610.30054 for its current degeneration ingredients, and the claimed lower bound, if correct, rules out any improvement. So the live work is verification: referee the November 2026 lower-bound preprint, or find the gap in it. Its Lean release states the division-free exponent and proves the square case with division, but the paper's explicit per-n bound is not formalized; formalizing it is a concrete, checkable task. The laser-method directions that drove progress to 2.371339 cannot reach the new record.
